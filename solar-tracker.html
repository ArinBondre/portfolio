Solar Tracker

An Arduino-based system that uses light sensors and servo motors to orient a solar panel toward brighter light.

Back to portfolio

Abstract

Aligning a solar panel with incoming light

This project explores a solar tracker that adjusts a panel's orientation in response to the direction of incoming light. Four light-dependent resistors (LDRs) provide directional readings, and an Arduino UNO uses the differences between those readings to control servo motors. The prototype combines sensing, embedded control, and a fabricated structure to help the panel follow brighter light.

01 · Project objectives

Use sensor feedback to orient the panel

The project explores closed-loop light sensing and mechanical adjustment, using the Arduino to respond to changes in the light measured around the panel.

  • Use an Arduino UNO to control the solar panel's orientation
  • Compare four LDR readings and adjust the panel with servo motors
  • Integrate the sensors, controller, servos, panel, and support structure

02 · System integration

Light sensors and panel movement

In the tracking prototype, four LDRs are arranged as directional sensors around the panel. Their readings are supplied to the Arduino, which drives the horizontal and vertical servos. The mechanical frame holds the panel and sensor assembly as it responds to changing light direction.

  • Controller: Arduino UNO for the tracking system
  • Light sensing: four LDRs arranged to compare light in the horizontal and vertical directions
  • Actuation: two servo motors for horizontal and vertical positioning
  • Structure: solar panel on a fabricated or 3D-printed support frame

03 · Software & math logic

Use sensor differences to guide movement

The controller compares LDR readings from opposite sides. The horizontal difference can be written as ΔH = Lright − Lleft, and the vertical difference as ΔV = Lupper − Llower. If either difference exceeds the system's balance tolerance, the matching servo adjusts the panel toward the side receiving more light. When the readings are close, the tracker can hold its position instead of continuing to move.

  • Horizontal error: compare the left and right LDR readings
  • Vertical error: compare the upper and lower LDR readings
  • Control response: use each difference to choose the direction of the corresponding servo adjustment
  • Balance: reduce unnecessary motion when paired readings are close

04 · Final outcome

A light-responsive solar tracking prototype

The project demonstrates a solar-tracking system in which LDR sensors provide directional feedback, an Arduino processes the readings, and servos reposition the panel. It brings mechanical construction and embedded control together in a renewable-energy prototype.

  • Four LDR sensors provide directional light readings
  • An Arduino UNO processes sensor differences
  • Servo motors reposition the solar panel in response

LDR readings can be affected by overcast conditions and surrounding light, while servo mechanisms are best suited to a small prototype.
